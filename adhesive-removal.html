        The problem
        
Desert sun turns adhesive into part of the glass

        
Fresh vinyl peels off in minutes. Signage that has been baking on a
        south facing storefront for five years is a completely different job, and it is the reason
        most people give up and start pricing new glass.

      

      

        
UV cross links it
Pressure sensitive adhesive hardens under sustained sun until it stops behaving like a removable residue and starts behaving like part of the surface.

        
The carrier lies to you
The printed vinyl peels away easily and looks like progress. The adhesive layer underneath is the actual job and it is still entirely there.

        
Wrong solvent, worse problem
Solvent aggressive enough for cured adhesive will also soften gaskets, lift paint and destroy window film if it is not kept off them.

        
Replacement is the expensive answer
A storefront pane runs several hundred dollars plus the glazier. Cleaning it is almost always worth trying properly first.

      

    

  


  

    

      

        How we actually do it
        
Three stages, least aggressive first

        
We escalate only where the previous stage failed to release the material.
        Each agent gets full dwell time, gets worked mechanically, then gets rinsed and assessed
        before the next one is tried.

      

      

        
Stage 1 · Citrus solvent
A low aggression citrus and oil based remover worked with non abrasive pads. The standard first pass for pressure sensitive adhesive, and it clears the majority of most storefronts on its own.

        
Stage 2 · Solvent remover
A stronger formulation, used only on what stage one would not release. Applied to the glass alone and kept clear of frames, gaskets and adjacent finishes.

        
Stage 3 · Mineral polish
A polishing treatment of the kind used on hard water etching, run to test whether a remaining haze is mineral in origin rather than adhesive at all.

        
Then the finish
Fresh blade work on wetted soaped glass, several grades of pad worked wet, and a full detergent wash and squeegee finish. Solvent leaves oil, and oil smears.

      

    

  


  

    

      

        When it is not the adhesive
        
Sometimes the glass is the problem

      

      

        

          If material liquefies under solvent and then redistributes across the pane instead of
          releasing from it, it is not sitting on top of the glass. It is bonded into the surface.
          That points to one of three things, and no cleaning process reverses any of them.
        

        
    
          
  • Cross linked adhesive fused to the glass after years of UV
    • 
          
  • A failed film or factory coating breaking down and smearing
    • 
          
  • Etched or abraded glass, where no residue is present at all and what you are seeing is the glass itself
    • 
        

        

          When we hit that, we stop. You are not billed for scrubbing that was never going to work,
          and you get a written condition report covering what was found, every method attempted and
          the result on each pane. Landlords and glaziers ask for exactly that before approving a
          replacement, and most cleaning companies cannot produce one.

        FAQ
        
Adhesive removal questions

      


      

        

          How much does adhesive removal cost?
          
Up to three panes starts at $129. A full storefront of up to ten pane faces starts at $289. Full coverage window graphics and wraps are quoted by the square foot because the area drives the labour. The price is firm before we start.

        

        

          Why is old adhesive so much harder than fresh?
          
Pressure sensitive adhesive cross links under UV. After a few High Desert summers it stops behaving like a residue that lifts and starts behaving like part of the surface. Fresh vinyl peels in minutes. Signage that has been baking for five years is a staged chemical job.

        

        

          What is your actual process?
          
Three stages, least aggressive first. A citrus based solvent with full dwell time clears most storefronts. Where that does not release we escalate to a stronger solvent formulation, applied to the glass only and kept off frames and gaskets. If a haze remains we run a mineral deposit remover with a polishing compound to test whether it is mineral rather than adhesive. Alongside that, fresh blade work on wetted soaped glass, several grades of pad, and a full wash and squeegee finish at the end.

        

        

          What if the adhesive will not come off?
          
Sometimes it is not adhesive at all. If material liquefies under solvent and redistributes instead of releasing, it is bonded into the glass surface rather than sitting on it, which usually means cross linked adhesive, a failed film or coating, or glass that is etched. In that case we stop, we do not bill for scrubbing that cannot work, and you get a written condition report setting out what was tried and what we found.

        

        

          Can you remove old window graphics and lettering?
          
Yes. Vinyl lettering, full coverage graphics, perforated window film, decals and price stickers. The printed carrier usually peels away easily. It is the adhesive layer underneath that is the actual job, and that is what the pricing is based on.

        

        

          Do you clean the glass afterwards?
          
Always. Every solvent leaves an oily film, and glass that is de-adhesived but not properly degreased will smear the first time it rains. Affected panes get a full detergent wash and squeegee finish as part of the price.
