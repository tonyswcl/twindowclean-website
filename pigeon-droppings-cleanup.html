        The cleanout
        
What comes out and how

      

      

        
Every panel edge is opened up and the nesting material comes out: sticks, feathers, eggs, dead birds when there are any, and the packed droppings underneath. It goes into bags on the roof and off the roof, not swept onto the yard. Then the roof deck, the tile and the panel undersides get scraped and washed.

        
We wear respirators for this and so should anyone doing it. Dried droppings go airborne when disturbed, and that dust is the part that is actually bad for you.

      

    

  


  

    

      

        What gets treated
        
Three places the mess ends up

      

      

        

          
Under the panels

          
The nests themselves and a layer of droppings on the roof deck that nobody can see from the ground. This is where the smell lives.

        

        

          
Tile and stucco

          
Droppings are acidic. Fresh ones wash off. A year old on concrete tile, some of the discoloration has gone into the surface, and we tell you what will not come back.

        

        

          
Driveway and patio

          
The white streaks under the eaves. Droppings on concrete and pavers come off with a pressure wash, quoted with the job.

        

      

    

  


  

    

      

        Sanitizing
        
Why washing is not enough

      

      

        
Washing moves the visible mess. Sanitizing kills what is left in the cracks of the tile and the grain of the roof deck, which is where the smell keeps coming from after a plain rinse. We treat the nesting area and the runoff path, then rinse. On a hot day the difference is obvious within a week.

        
Cleaning up the droppings and sanitizing the affected areas under and around the panels is included with pigeon proofing. A whole roof soft wash is from $599, with the final price quoted for your roof.

      

    

  


  

    

      

        Then keep them out
        
A cleanout without proofing is temporary

      

      

        
Pigeons come back to a roost, and the ones that hatched under your panels think of your roof as home. Cleaning without closing the array off buys a few weeks. That is why we do not sell the cleanout on its own: it is part of pigeon proofing, with rigid mesh clipped to the panel frame and 2 to 3 free spinners so they do not settle again.

        
$450 covers up to 12 panels, $50 a panel after that, cleanout, mesh, spinners, panel wash and cleanup of the droppings under and around the panels included. Include your panel count in the service request so Tony can confirm pricing.

        FAQ
        
Droppings cleanup questions

      

      

        

          Do you clean up the droppings or just install mesh?
          
Both, always. The nests, droppings and debris come out from under every panel and get hauled off, the area is sanitized, then the mesh goes on. Mesh over a dirty roof seals the smell in.

        

        

          Will the smell go away?
          
Yes, once the nesting material is out and the area is sanitized. Washing alone leaves it in the tile and the roof deck. If a smell lingers after a hot week, call and we come back.

        

        

          Will the stains on my tile come off?
          
Fresh droppings come off completely. Droppings that have sat on concrete tile for a year or more have etched into the surface a little, and some shadow can remain. We tell you before we start what to expect.

        

        

          Is pigeon droppings dust dangerous?
          
Disturbed dry droppings can carry things you do not want to breathe, which is why we wear respirators for the cleanout and bag everything on the roof instead of sweeping it off. You do not need to be home for it and you do not want to be under it.

        

        

          Can I just have the cleanout without the mesh?
          
We do not offer it on its own, because the birds come back within weeks and you pay twice. The cleanout is part of pigeon proofing, which closes the array off and keeps them away.
