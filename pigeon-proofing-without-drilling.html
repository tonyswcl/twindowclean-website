Pigeon Proofing Without Drilling

Rigid steel mesh, clipped to the lip of the panel frame. No screws, no bolts, no holes in your panels or your roof.

There are two ways to hold pigeon mesh on a solar array. One drills or screws it into the panel frame or the roof. The other clips it to the frame's lip. The clipped way costs a little more in parts and takes more care to fit. It is the only way we do it, and this page is why.

Clips or screws

What each one does to your roof

Screws through the frame

Every hole is a place water gets in and a reason for a solar company to deny a warranty claim. In High Desert wind the mesh works against the screw heads and loosens within a couple of seasons.

Screws into the roof

Holes through tile or shingle underlayment, right next to the array where a leak is hardest to find. Nobody wants to explain that to a roofer.

Clips on the frame lip

A steel clip grips the inner lip of the aluminum frame and pins the mesh to it. No penetration anywhere. It holds in wind, and it comes off clean if a panel ever needs service.

The mesh

What we clip on

Rigid, rust resistant steel mesh, not plastic and not chicken wire. Chicken wire rusts, sags and gets pushed aside by a bird that wants in. Plastic goes brittle in the sun. Steel mesh cut to the array and clipped every few inches stays flat and stays put.

The mesh runs the full perimeter, including the corners and the gap at the top edge where installers leave room for airflow. Pigeons find a two inch gap in a day, so the perimeter gets walked twice before we leave.

Warranty

Why your installer will be fine with it

Panel and installer warranties cover the module and the mounting. Drilling into either one gives them a reason to walk away from a claim. Clips touch the frame lip and nothing else, so there is nothing to argue about. If a panel ever has to come out for service, the clips release and go back on afterward.

We include a lifetime workmanship warranty of our own, in writing, on every job. It is for the original customer and does not transfer if the property is sold. If mesh or clips fail because of how we installed them, we repair or replace the affected mesh and clips at no charge. It does not cover storm damage or birds getting in through areas that were not originally protected, and removal or changes by anyone else void it for the whole installation.

The rest of the job

What comes with the mesh

The birds are cleaned out first: nests, eggs, droppings and debris hauled off, and the area sanitized so the smell goes. Then the mesh, then 2 to 3 reflective spinners on the roof vents, held with hose clamps, no drilling there either. The panels get washed with purified water and the droppings where the birds were are cleaned up and sanitized, both included. A whole roof wash is a separate service.

        FAQ
        
Clips and drilling questions

      

      

        

          Do you drill into the panels or the roof?
          
No. The mesh is held by steel clips that grip the inner lip of the panel frame. Spinners mount on the roof vents with hose clamps. Nothing gets a hole.

        

        

          Do the clips hold in the wind?
          
Yes. The clip pins the mesh against the frame lip, so wind pushes the mesh into the clip rather than away from it. Screwed mesh is the kind that works loose, because the mesh flexes against the screw heads until they strip.

        

        

          Will this affect my solar warranty?
          
Clips touch the frame lip and nothing else, so there is no penetration for a manufacturer or installer to point at. Screws through the frame or the roof are exactly what they point at.

        

        

          What if a panel needs service later?
          
The clips release by hand, the mesh section comes away, the panel is serviced, and the mesh goes back on. Nothing to patch afterward.

        

        

          Is chicken wire ever okay?
          
No. It rusts, it sags, and a pigeon pushes it aside. Rigid steel mesh cut to the array is the only material we install.
