        The job
        
What happens on the roof

      

      

        

          
Cleanout first

          
Nests, droppings and feathers come out from under the array and go in the truck, and the area is sanitized so the smell leaves with the birds. On a colony that has been there a few seasons this is the biggest part of the job.

        

        

          
Mesh clipped, never drilled

          
Rigid, rust resistant steel mesh, not chicken wire, held by clips that grip the lip of the panel frame. No screws into the frame or the roof, so your panel manufacturer warranty stays as it was.

        

        

          
Spinners and a wash

          
Two or three reflective spinners go on the ridge or the array edge, free. Then the panels get a purified water wash and the droppings under and around them are cleaned up and sanitized, both included. A whole roof wash is a separate service.

        

      

    

  


  

    

      

        Antelope Valley wind
        
Why clips matter more out here

      

      

        
The Antelope Valley is one of the windiest places in Southern California, and a sheet of mesh loads like a sail. Mesh screwed into the frame holds until it has flexed against the screw a few thousand times, then the hole opens up and a corner starts flapping. A clip pins the mesh against the frame lip across its whole width, so the harder the wind pushes, the tighter it sits. It also comes off clean if a panel ever needs service, which is something the solar companies out here ask about.
