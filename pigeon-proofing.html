      What spinners cover, and what they don't

      They do
      • Flash sunlight and keep moving in the wind. Pigeons stay away from light and motion they can't predict.
      • Keep birds off the open roof, the ridge and the eaves, where they land and walk.
      • Cover the side of the roof they face. The flash travels in a straight line.
      • Mount on your roof vents with stainless hose clamps. No drilling into the roof.
      They don't
      • Clear a nest that's already under your panels. The cleanout and the mesh do that.
      • See over the ridge. One spinner on the front leaves the back side open, and the pigeons just move there.
      • Replace the mesh. Mesh is the guarantee, spinners are the deterrent.
      Why It Matters

      Pigeons under solar panels aren't just noisy

      The gap between your panels and the roof is warm, shaded and hidden from hawks. Out here that is exactly what a pigeon is looking for. Once a pair nests, the flock follows.

      • ⚡ Your panels stop producing what you're paying for Droppings and nesting debris shade cells and bake on in 100°+ heat. A fouled array doesn't produce the numbers you financed it on.
      • 🧱 Droppings eat roofing and hardware Pigeon droppings are acidic. Left on tile, shingle, flashing and panel mounts, they corrode and stain, and the damage is not reversible with a rinse.
      • 🔥 Nesting material is dry tinder next to live wiring Twigs, feathers and dried droppings packed against conduit and junction boxes are exactly what you don't want on a High Desert roof in August.
      • 🐾 It ends up in your yard, your pool and your dog Every rain and every wind moves droppings off the roof and down. Pets that get into it get sick, and pool chemistry pays for it too.
      • 😷 Dried droppings turn into airborne dust This is why we sanitize instead of sweeping, and why we bag and haul everything. Dry-brushing a fouled array just puts it in the air you breathe.
      • 👃 The smell nobody talks about Droppings and old nests under the panels give off a sharp ammonia smell that gets worse in summer heat and drifts into the yard. We haul it all away and sanitize the area, so the smell leaves with the birds.
      • 📈 It gets worse, never better Pigeons return to a successful nest site and breed year-round in this climate. The job is always cheaper before the flock triples.

      Know Your Enemy

      Why pigeons choose solar panels

      The gap between a solar panel and the roof runs about four to six inches. It's shaded from the sun, sheltered from wind, warmed from above by the panel and from below by the roof, and almost nothing can reach a bird once it's in there. If you set out to design a nesting site for a pigeon, you'd build exactly that.

      This is why the problem is so common on newer homes. Rooftop solar became standard on California new construction, and nobody installing it was thinking about birds. A neighborhood built in the last fifteen years is a neighborhood full of ideal nesting sites, and pigeons are very good at finding them.

      They also don't leave. Pigeons return to the same nesting site season after season, and their offspring come back to the site they were raised in. That's why hosing it out or chasing them off buys a few months at most. Until the gap is physically closed, you have a permanent tenant.

      The Alternatives, Honestly

      What about spikes, netting, gel and spinners?

      Here is the straight answer on each.

      • 📌 Spikes, right product in the wrong place Spikes deter birds from landing on a flat open surface like a ledge or parapet. They do nothing about the gap under your panels, because the birds aren't landing there. They walk in from the side.
      • 🕸️ Netting, built for commercial work Netting works on large commercial structures where the whole area gets enclosed. On a residential array it looks poor, sags in the wind, and traps debris.
      • 🧴 Gels and repellents, temporary by design They degrade in UV and need reapplication, and desert sun is hard on them. You'd be buying the same fix twice a year.
      • ✨ Reflective spinners, included free on every job Spinners flash and turn in the wind, which discourages birds from settling on the open roof around the array and takes pressure off the mesh. They don't close the gap under the panels, so on their own they are never the fix. Paired with the mesh they are worth having, which is why every pigeon proofing job includes 2 to 3 installed free, depending on the size of the home.
      • 🔒 Physical exclusion, the lasting answer Close the gap and there's nothing to argue about. That's why mesh secured with clips is the core of every job we do, backed by our lifetime workmanship warranty.
      Timing

      When to do it

      Any time is better than waiting, but fall and winter are easiest. Pigeons nest in spring, roughly February through April. Exclusion done in autumn means they never establish for the coming season. Wait until April and we're removing active nests with eggs or chicks in them, which is slower, messier and harder on the birds. Reading this in the fall? This is the right time.

      FAQ

      Pigeon proofing questions

      Why are pigeons nesting under my solar panels?

      The gap between your panels and the roof is warm, shaded and hidden from hawks, which is exactly what a pigeon looks for out here. Once a pair nests, the flock follows. Mesh clipped around the array closes that gap, and that is what actually stops it.

      Do you install bird spinners?

      Yes, and they are free. Every pigeon proofing job includes 2 to 3 reflective spinners, depending on the size of the home. They flash and turn in the wind and make the open roof less inviting, which takes pressure off the mesh. The mesh is what keeps pigeons out from under the panels. The spinners make them less likely to land on the roof in the first place.

      How much does pigeon proofing cost?

      Most solar array jobs start at $450 and are priced by panel count and roof access. That includes removing old nests and mesh, full dropping cleanup and sanitizing, and installing new mesh secured with clips. The inspection is free and the quote is firm before we start.

      Why do you use clips instead of bolts?

      Drilling bolts through the mesh puts holes in the material and, on some installs, in the panel frame, which can void your solar warranty. The mesh works itself off those bolts in High Desert wind and the pigeons walk right back in. Stainless clips grip the panel frame without penetrating it, hold in wind, and come off cleanly if a panel ever needs service.

      Do you remove the droppings and nests too?

      Yes, and it is included rather than an upcharge. We remove the nests, bag and haul the debris, wash and sanitize the affected roof and panel area, then rinse the panels. You should never be left with cleanup after a pigeon proofing job.

      Is pigeon proofing humane?

      Yes. We don't trap, poison or harm the birds. We remove the nesting habitat, seal the gap under the array, and add deterrents so the flock relocates and stops treating your roof as home.

      How long does pigeon proofing take?

      Most residential solar arrays are finished in a single day. Larger arrays, heavy nesting or difficult roof access can run into a second day, and we tell you that at the inspection, not after we've started.

      What if the pigeons come back?

      Our pigeon proofing carries a lifetime workmanship warranty. It is for the original customer and does not transfer if the property is sold. If mesh or clips fail because of how we installed them, we repair or replace the affected mesh and clips at no charge. It does not cover storm damage or birds getting in through areas that were not originally protected, and removal or changes by anyone else void it for the whole installation.

      Will the mesh void my solar warranty?

      No. We clip to the panel frame and never drill into panels, penetrate the roof or touch the electrical system. If your installer requires notification, tell us and we'll document the work for them.

      Can I just clean out the nesting myself?

      We'd rather you didn't. Dried droppings and nesting dust are a respiratory hazard, and the work happens on a roof, around live DC wiring, often on a slope. It's also only half the job if the gap stays open.

      What if my panels are leased or under warranty?

      Exclusion work of this kind is generally fine, but check your agreement. We'll provide photos and a description of the work for your provider.
