        The High Desert Difference
        
Windows get dirty faster here, and it is not dirt

        
It's minerals and wind. Understanding both is why our cleanings last.

      


      
    
        
  • 
          
          Hard water is the thing most people don't know they have
          Water in this region carries a heavy mineral load. Every time a sprinkler catches the bottom of a window, the water evaporates and leaves the minerals behind. A few hundred repeats and the deposit stops being a spot you can wipe off. It starts etching into the glass itself. That cloudy band along the lower third that no amount of Windex touches? It's not dirty. It's damaged.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
          Then there's the wind
          Open desert, wide gaps between developments, and the Cajon Pass funneling air up from below. Fine grit rides that wind, works into screens and tracks, and gets driven back onto the glass every time it picks up. A house cleaned in March can look untouched by May, which is why regular service costs less here than infrequent deep cleans.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          
          Why we always do the screens
          Leaving grit in a screen puts it straight back on the glass at the next breeze. Companies that skip screens are doing half a job, and the result shows within a week. Screens, tracks and sills are in every exterior clean we do.
        
    •

        Hard Water Stain Removal
        
When cleaning isn't enough

        

          If your glass has gone cloudy near the base, a standard wash won't fix it. That needs
          mineral restoration, a different process using a compound that lifts the
          deposit off the surface. It takes longer and costs more, and it is what actually lifts the deposit.
        

      

      

        

          We'll tell you honestly which one your windows need before quoting, and we'll tell you if the
          etching has gone too deep to fully recover. Some glass is past saving, and you deserve to know
          that rather than pay for a treatment that disappoints.
        

        

          Once it's clean, adjust your sprinklers. It is the most useful thing you can do
          for your windows and it costs nothing.
        

        

          Restoration is priced from $12 a pane with a free test pane first. The full explanation, including how we tell
          stage one from stage two, is on the hard water removal page.
          New construction and remodel glass with stucco, paint and stickers on it is a different job again, covered under
          post construction window cleaning.

        FAQ
        
Window cleaning questions

      


      

        

          How much does window cleaning cost?
          
Single-story exterior starts at $149, two-story at $249, with screens, tracks and sills included. Interior is an add-on. Hard water restoration is quoted after we see the glass, because the price depends on how far the etching has gone.

        

        

          Do you clean window screens?
          
Yes, included in every exterior clean along with tracks and sills. Leaving grit in a screen undoes the window.

        

        

          What is the cloudy area at the bottom of my windows?
          
Hard water etching from sprinkler overspray. Standard cleaning won't remove it. It needs mineral restoration, and it's worth catching early. Once it's clean, adjust your sprinklers, which costs nothing and prevents it coming back.

        

        

          Do you use soap?
          
No. Purified, deionized water, which dries without spots and leaves no residue for dust to cling to.

        

        

          Can you reach second floor windows?
          
Yes. Water-fed poles reach safely from the ground, with ladder work where needed. Two-story is standard for us, not a special request.

        

        

          How often should windows be cleaned in the High Desert?
          
Twice a year for most High Desert homes: spring after the wind, fall before the holidays. Quarterly on corner lots, near open land, or where sprinklers hit the glass.

        

        

          Do I need to be home?
          
Not for exterior work, as long as gates are unlocked and pets are secured. Interior work needs someone there.
